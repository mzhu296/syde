Preprocessing: mean-centered; not standardized to unit variance.

Method comparison
PCA is linear, has a closed-form eigendecomposition/SVD solution, supplies an explained-variance compression measure, and supports inverse transformation and reconstruction-error analysis. Isomap instead builds a nearest-neighbor graph and attempts to preserve graph-geodesic distances, so it can unroll a curved manifold. It does not provide a PCA-style explained-variance measure or a simple inverse reconstruction.

Quantitative comparison
A 5-neighbor classifier was evaluated on each completed 2D embedding using stratified 10-fold cross-validation. Because the embeddings were fitted to the full dataset before validation, these scores are descriptive rather than a leakage-free estimate of an end-to-end prediction pipeline.

The setosa points form their own connected component. The graph stays disconnected for every k tried (3, 5, 10), because setosa is separated from the other two species by a gap larger than its k-nearest-neighbor links can bridge. sklearn joins disconnected components with extra edges before the geodesic computation, so setosa is compressed into a thin strip in the embedding and its internal spread is not preserved; increasing k changes that shape but does not remove the gap.

Isomap's reconstruction_error() is measured between geodesic-distance kernels, so it is not on the same scale as PCA's data-space error; the pairwise-distance stress is the common yardstick.

Conclusion
The Iris structure is already close to linear: both embeddings show a cleanly separated setosa cluster and partial versicolor/virginica overlap. Isomap gains little here. PCA is the better compression choice because its two-dimensional representation retains 97.76% of the variance, is directly invertible, and has a meaningful reconstruction error.
